Exercise
✦•┈๑⋅⋯ ⋯⋅๑┈•✦

Regular exercise helps maintain healthy weight, reduces risk of cardiac disease and diabetes. Being physically active is important not only for physical health, but also mental health. According to the CDC, regular physical activity is linked to lower rates of depression and anxiety. Additionally, exercise can be a good way to build community. Being on a school sports team or having regular group workouts increases social interaction. See the exercise dictionary in the sources below for workout ideas.


While all types of exercise are beneficial, activities that target muscle strength are extremely important, especially for aging adults. Strength training, like lifting, using resistance bands, and , not only build muscle, but also slow bone loss. After age 40, research shows bone mass decreases 1% each year, leading to conditions like osteoporosis. An estimated 10 million Americans suffer from osteoporosis. By focusing on strength training, you can decrease your chance of injury.

Sleep
✦•┈๑⋅⋯ ⋯⋅๑┈•✦

Sleep is essential to a multitude of bodily functions, including but not limited to, circulatory system health, hormones, metabolic function, respiratory health, immune system health, and brain functions involving memory and focus. Sleep is crucial for development in adolescents, lacking sleep can cause detrimental consequences in later life (NIH). Furthermore, sleep has been shown to lower risk of chronic illnesses like high blood pressure, stroke, type 2 diabetes, and heart disease.

Tips to Getting Better Sleep...
- Set a consistent time to go to bed each night and wake up each morning. Building a sleep schedule will help your brain associate times with sleeping and help you to get a better night’s sleep.
- Keep your sleeping environment cool.
- Avoid caffeine prior to bed, stop consuming caffeine at least 6-8 hours before sleeping. Caffeine is meant to block tired signals from your brain, making falling asleep incredibly difficult.
- Avoid using screens for at least 30 minutes before bed. (CDC)
